# Ancient Memphis Visual Reference Scope

Purpose: collect internet-viewable visual references for how other historians, archaeologists, artists, museums, and educational publishers imagine ancient Memphis or comparable Old Kingdom Egyptian urban life.

Important: this file is for human art-direction review only. Do not copy these images into runtime assets, upload them into generators, train/fine-tune on them, or treat them as direct evidence unless rights and source status are reviewed separately.

## Early Takeaways For The Hero Street

- Direct Memphis reconstructions are scarce. The best city-scale reference is Golvin’s hypothetical Memphis, not a precise Early Dynastic / Old Kingdom plan.
- The strongest project-specific research cluster is AERA/Mit Rahina/Kom el-Fakhry, especially “Memphis, a City Unseen” and the Memphis project page.
- For street and house detail, Giza’s Old Kingdom worker settlement is a better analog than later temple-monument images.
- Ordinary residential walls should continue to read as mudbrick core with plastered/whitewashed earthen surface, irregular wear, and small openings.
- Market shade/awning details are plausible, but should remain restrained: linen/reed shade cloth and simple wooden/reed supports, not elaborate later-period canopies.
- Temple-of-Ptah images online are often much later period; useful for distant endpoint mood, not for our early domestic street.
- Use Old Kingdom tomb reliefs for people, clothing, work poses, baskets, pottery, animal handling, bread/beer production, and market/craft life.
