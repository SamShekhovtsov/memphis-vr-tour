ANCIENT MEMPHIS - BUSINESS PLAN RESEARCH AND MODEL NOTES
Prepared 22 October 2026. Currency: US dollars.

Purpose
A short investor business plan with 24 monthly forecasts after a commercially
ready VR pilot/MVP. The existing browser prototype is not that paid VR MVP.
No launch calendar date, budget, team roster, signed clients or paid pilot data
were supplied. The user requested USD and delegated market/model choices.

Evidence versus assumptions
The research establishes that ticketed cultural VR, institutional content
licensing and relevant cultural audiences exist. It cannot establish actual
future sales of Ancient Memphis. Every forward customer, usage, price, cost,
retention and cash figure is therefore explicitly conditional. Scenarios have
no assigned probabilities and are not statistical confidence intervals.

Why this model
Initial commercial focus is a US English-language venue channel, with VR as
the flagship and no forecast browser revenue. A $10 net-before-sales-tax
visitor ticket is a pricing hypothesis below the two general-admission US
benchmarks. Company licensing is assumed to be $250/site/month + $2.50/net
paid visit, plus $500 delivered onboarding. These licensing fees require
direct buyer testing; they are not represented as market-average rates.

The 200-account qualified prospect target is a proposed research/sales workload,
not a verified serviceable market or an existing pipeline. Base funnel:
Year 1: 100 qualified prospects -> 40 demos -> 16 trials -> 8 wins.
Year 2: 100 qualified prospects -> 50 demos -> 28 trials -> 14 wins.
This assumes roughly 30 raw accounts contacted monthly, 60-120 day sales cycles
and evidence/referrals improving Year 2 conversion. The 50% trial-close ratio
is a model assumption, not published sector data.

Base has 22 paying sites acquired and two Year 1 accounts not renewed at their
12-month anniversaries (M15, M21); 20 remain in M24. Each live site's first two
months operate at 50% then 75% of mature usage. Mature base usage is 240 net
paid sessions/month, compared with a 1,200 monthly capacity ceiling:
4 headsets x 3 twenty-minute slots/hour x 5 hours/day x 20 days.
At a proposed qualifying venue with 10,000 monthly visitors, 240 sessions
would require 2.4% uptake. Neither 10,000 footfall nor 2.4% uptake is measured
for an identified buyer. Selection must verify footfall and actual take-up.

Scenarios
Conservative: 10 gross wins, 2 losses, 8 end sites, 120 mature visits/month.
Base: 22 gross wins, 2 losses, 20 end sites, 240 mature visits/month.
Upside: 37 gross wins, 3 losses, 34 end sites, 360 mature visits/month.
Conservative and base fixed spending: $18,000/month Y1, $22,000/month Y2.
Upside fixed spending: $18,000/month Y1, $26,000/month Y2 for added capacity.
No calendar seasonality, price inflation or automatic growth in same-site
usage after the ramp is assumed. The downside is not a cost-reduction plan.

Revenue, profit and cash
Venue ticket sales are NOT startup revenue. Model JSON reports both separately.
Paid sessions are not de-duplicated people. Paying sites are locations; one
institution could own more than one, so these are not necessarily unique
legal customer entities. No consumer storefront, school subscription, grant,
sponsorship, custom commission or acquisition income is included.

Direct expenses = $60 per live site + $0.25 per net paid visit + $300 per new
site + 8% of company revenue. The 8% is a planning reserve: 3% for payment/
collection costs and 5% for engine/distribution contingency. It is not an
assertion that a particular engine charges 5% of the startup's licence revenue.
Applicable terms and potentially different gross-revenue bases need review.
Fixed costs include distributed/part-time production, founder-led business
development, QA/history, sales/travel, admin/tools and general contingency.
No fully staffed US development studio is implied by this lean budget.

Operating result = company revenue minus fixed and direct costs. It is before
tax, interest and depreciation and includes the budget reserve above; it is
a management planning result, not audited accounting profit.
Revenue collections lag one month. All operating expenses are paid in the
month incurred. Demo/test equipment costs $15,000 in M1 and $3,000 in M13.
Prior MVP development, sales taxes, debt/financing and company income tax are
outside the model. Venue-owned systems are outside startup capex.

Base reconciliation
Year 1 company revenue: $34,550; Year 2: $145,200; total: $179,750.
Net paid visits: 8,220 + 38,280 = 46,500.
24-month operating deficit: $345,455.
Equipment cash spending: $18,000; ending receivables: $17,050.
Peak cash need: $380,505. With 20% buffer, rounded upward to $5,000: $460,000.
Base mature recurring site revenue: $850; direct costs: $188;
contribution: $662. Ceiling($22,000/$662) = 34 mature sites to break even.
This exceeds the base end count; no base break-even date is asserted.
Upside first positive operating month is M22 but remains cumulatively negative.

Venue feasibility
At 240 visits x $10 = $2,400, less $850 licence/usage, $72 payment cost,
$800 incremental shared staffing, $250 equipment allocation and $100 other
incremental overhead = $328/month contribution before existing rent/tax.
Equipment allocation assumes $12,000 / 48 months; obtain hardware quotes.
The venue buys equipment; the startup's demo/test capex is separate.
A dedicated 100-hour attendant rather than 40 incremental shared hours adds
$1,200 and turns $328 into -$872. Model requires shared staffing/existing space.
Variable margin is $10 - $2.50 - $0.30 = $7.20; fixed venue costs $1,400.
Venue break-even is ceiling($1,400/$7.20) = 195 paid visits/month.

Investment framing
$460,000 is a conditional post-MVP planning envelope, not a valuation or a
verified fundraising requirement. Proposed staged review: $150,000 for M1-M6,
then $310,000 only against usage, sales and operational evidence. Initial VR
production must be costed separately before claiming an end-to-end capital ask.
Best initial fit may be strategic operators, cultural-tech angels and impact
capital. No VC return multiple or exit promise is invented.
